Featured Overview

Platform Overview

What is Gofer AI?

Gofer is an embodied AI and spatial data platform that pays you to record short videos of everyday tasks — things like pouring a glass of water, folding a towel, or stacking plates. Those recordings become high-value training demonstrations that teach next-generation robots how to perform useful, real-world tasks with safe, human-level dexterity. By transforming everyday iPhones into intuitive data collection tools, Gofer bridges the physical data bottleneck in robotics while directly rewarding contributors for their time and data.

Getting Started & Tasks

Q: How does it work?

Getting started as a contributor takes just three simple steps:

  1. Open the app and pick a task from the available list.
  2. Follow the on-screen prompt and record a short video clip (usually under a couple of minutes).
  3. Submit it. Once it's accepted and verified by our system, you get paid.

Q: What do I need to get started?

A: Just your phone. No special equipment. You'll set up a free account and complete a quick one-time verification before your first submission.

Q: What phones are supported?

A: Gofer is available on iPhone. Recording uses your phone's camera and motion sensors, so it runs on a real device (not an emulator).

Q: Do I have to be 18?

A: Yes. Gofer is for adults only (18+), and there's a quick one-time age and identity check before you can submit your first clip.

Earnings & Payouts

Q: How much can I earn, and how do I get paid?

A: Each task shows its pay before you record, so you always know what a clip is worth. Payouts are handled securely through Stripe — you connect a bank account once and manage your balance and transfers from there.

Data Ownership & Privacy

Q: Who owns my recordings?

A: You do. When you submit a clip, you choose which kinds of uses it can be licensed for. You stay in control of your own data.

Q: What are my recordings used for?

A: They become training examples that help robots learn everyday hand skills. Some uses are always off-limits, no matter what — military, defence, and surveillance are never permitted.

Q: Is my privacy protected?

A: Yes. You decide what to record and which uses you allow, and we handle your data with care. Full details are in our Privacy Policy and Contributor Terms, linked from the app and this site.

Robotics & Technology

Q: What is a Semantic VLA?

A: A Semantic Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model is a neural network architecture that integrates visual perception, natural language understanding, and physical action generation into a unified system. Gofer AI's platform is built around enhancing VLA models with high-quality, real-world semantic demonstration data.

Q: How does RAG apply to robotics?

A: Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) in robotics allows a robot's control system to query a structured memory of past demonstrations, successful actions, or semantic knowledge at runtime. Rather than relying solely on pre-trained weights, a RAG-enabled robot can retrieve relevant past experience to make better decisions in novel situations.

Q: What is the "data bottleneck"?

A: The data bottleneck refers to the chronic shortage of high-quality, real-world training data that limits the performance and generalizability of robotic AI systems. Unlike language models which can be trained on internet text, robots need precise, semantically labeled sensorimotor data that is extremely expensive and time-consuming to collect at scale. Solving this bottleneck is Gofer AI's core mission.

Q: What are imitation learning pipelines?

A: Imitation learning (IL) pipelines allow robots to learn behaviors by observing and replicating human demonstrations, rather than through hand-coded rules or pure reinforcement learning. Common techniques include Behavior Cloning (BC) and Dataset Aggregation (DAgger). Gofer AI's platform makes high-quality IL data collection and curation scalable and accessible.

Q: How do spatial AI models work?

A: Spatial AI models process multi-modal sensory inputs — such as RGB video, depth data, and motion sensors — to build a geometric and semantic understanding of 3D environments. These models enable robots to reason about object positions, scene layouts, and physical affordances critical for manipulation and navigation.
